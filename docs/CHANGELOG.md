# Change Log

This file records completed user-visible, operational, architectural, packaging, and documentation changes. Keep unreleased entries concise and move short-term execution detail to `TODO.md`.

## Unreleased

### Added
- Phones send their own diagnostic log to each paired computer every 2 minutes (and 30 seconds after the app starts). The computer keeps the newest copy and serves it at `http://127.0.0.1:55432/api/v1/diagnostics/peers/<device id>/log`, so nobody has to copy a log off a phone. Lines are redacted on the phone and again on the computer, and are capped in number and length.
- **Computers log every incoming session** (Diagnostic log, area `session`): which device said hello and from where, whether it proved who it is or why it was refused, the first message of each kind it sent (with how many readings), every refusal sent back, and how the session ended (closed, dropped, nothing heard, sending failed) with totals. Phones and computers also log, per device, how many of their readings the other side already has and how many they are sending, and when a session goes live.
- **Diagnostic log screen** (More > Diagnostic log) on every device, with **Copy**: what the device did with pairing, each connection attempt (address, result and reason), removals and update checks. Phones now log every connection step, including a connection loop that stopped and why, which they used to swallow.
- **One way to add a device.** Every phone and tablet has one **Scan a code** button that takes any pairing code: a computer's code pairs over the network and waits for Accept there, and a phone's or tablet's code goes to the face-to-face check. Adding, seeing and removing devices are on one screen, **Add or remove devices**, on every device; the separate "Pair a device" page and the "Scan the other device" button are gone, and typing a code or pasting an invitation sits under "Other ways".
- A paired device's own page now says, under its name, whether this device reaches it right now and why not (the same line as on its pairing card), and has a **Pairing and removal** button that opens its card in one tap. No more going through More to find out whether a device is connected.
- Service diagnosis (D6): alerts "Tailscale is not connected" (5 min), "Syncthing folder has errors" (5 min) and "Syncthing folders not in sync" (2 h), each with a plain explanation. Syncthing alerts have a **Rescan Syncthing** button that asks the computer's own Syncthing to rescan every folder (loopback only, antiforgery token, no files changed).
- The Map shows the name a found device has in Things.
- Quick capture from a model name or product link (D7): the kind and maker are guessed from the words (data table `Library/recognize.json`, about 35 kinds and 65 makers), marked estimated; a link becomes the name and is kept as the link. Nothing is fetched.
- **The app fills itself in.** Each computer now describes its own hardware (maker, model, processor, memory, graphics, drives, and which systems it can start: on the Fedora side "Fedora (running now), Windows Boot Manager"), the services it runs (Tailscale connected or not, Syncthing folders up to date, syncing or in error), and every device on your tailnet. All of it is measured and shows in Things and on the Map.
- **Find devices on this network** (Things, top of the page): a switch per computer, off until you turn it on. Once on, the computer finds the router, switch, printers, TVs, speakers, phones and other devices on its own home network (neighbor table, mDNS, UPnP, local names, a slow ping of its own subnet only), with names, makers and models. They appear on the Map under the router and as things linked "connected to" it, marked as discovered; nothing found is ever trusted.
- Tailscale and Syncthing appear on the Map as their own nodes beside the computer that runs them. The desktop app hands your Syncthing key to the Monitor App on the same computer so it can read folder status; the key never leaves the computer.
- Pairing you can trust (R1 to R6). Remove asks, works and answers on the device's own card. Each device card says whether this device is connected to it right now, through which address, since when, when its last reading arrived, and why it can't connect in plain words. A bar at the bottom answers every action ("Approving Laptop…", then the result or the reason it failed).
- A removed device can pair again (R3): scan the code as usual and approve it, and it is back on every device. Removing it again still takes effect everywhere at once, whatever the device clocks say. Details in `docs/DECISIONS.md` (R3).
- Phones without a fingerprint use the screen lock (PIN, pattern or password) to remove or add devices, and the Devices page says which one it will ask for.
- Navigation: the side rail labels every icon, the first mode is called Home, and More opens on pairing; "Overview" is now "About this device".
- Projects have a parts list (T084, P1 and P2). A project card in Things lists everything linked "Part of" it or put inside it, each marked to buy, ordered or in hand, with a line like "1 of 5 in hand, 1 ordered, 3 to buy" and what the rest costs ("To buy: $153.50 (1 price unknown)"). Totals stay per currency and nothing is converted; estimated prices say "about".
- Typed values named Price ($45, 39.99 EUR), Quantity (2), Width and Depth (70 mm) and Rack units (2U) are now stored as real values with units, so parts lists, racks and boards use them. Other names stay your own text values.
- A thing quick-added as owned can be moved back to planned or ordered, so something you still have to buy can be marked that way.

### Fixed
- Phone `[data]` lines report the app's own CPU time per minute and whether the screen is on, to measure how much of the battery drain is the app's work and whether reads continue with the screen off.
- Phone `[data]` lines also report process memory, and every 10 minutes the heap after a full collection, to tell a memory leak from churn (Android's runtime counts uncollected garbage in the plain heap figure). Each minute's Things bridge pass logs what it created and how many model changes exist (`[bridge]`).
- **Phones and tablets keep monitoring after an update or restart.** Before, an update closed monitoring and nothing started it again until you tapped Start monitoring, so the device went silent. Now, once you start it, it resumes by itself after updates, reboots and when you open the app, until you tap Stop. (After installing this version, tap Start monitoring once more so it is remembered.)
- After a native crash or a freeze on a phone or tablet, the next start sends Android's own report once (`[exit]` lines: libraries, functions and the abort message), because such crashes never reach the app's crash catcher. On 2026-11-25 the Pixel's monitoring stopped at 21:31:46 UTC because of a native crash (Android exit reason 5).
- To find why phone monitoring keeps stopping: a restarted device now also sends the end of its previous run's log, plus Android's own record of why earlier runs ended (`[exit]`: crash, low memory, killed by the system, stopped by the person), and logs when the monitoring service is asked to stop, destroyed, swiped away or asked to free memory.
- Phones log once a minute how late the screen thread is (`[ui]`), and which data reads are slow, how long they waited for a thread, and memory clean-up pauses (`[data]`), to find the cause of the lag.
- After a phone or tablet app crash, the next start also writes the problem into the log the device sends to its computers (`[crash]` lines), so a crash can be read without a screenshot.
- Phones log, once a minute, how many screen refreshes ran and how long each part took (`[ui]` lines in the log they send to their computers), so app lag can be measured on the device.
- Phone app lag and flickering controls once readings flow: every live refresh rebuilt the selected computer's controls and opened two new sessions to it (update status, pairing requests), about five a second. The same selection now keeps its controls and re-reads at most every 30 s.
- **When the Monitor App service isn't running, the window says so and can start it.** On 2026-11-25 the laptop restarted, Windows gave up waiting 30 seconds for the service during a busy boot (event 7009), and Windows never retries a failed start, so nothing was recorded or shared until someone found it in Windows Services. Now every page says "The Monitor App service on this computer isn't running" and offers **Start the service** (Windows asks to allow it once). The Windows service is also published precompiled (ReadyToRun), so it starts faster.
- **Phones and computers share readings again.** The 0.1.33 logs showed why the Pixel never shared: on every connection it first downloaded the laptop's entire history (508,000 readings, oldest first, about 2,500 a minute), and only after that would it send its own readings and switch to live updates. Sessions dropped every few minutes, so it never got there. Now a phone sends its own readings first, fetches only the newest 20,000 of a computer's readings when it is behind (about the last 1.7 hours; older ones stay on the computer and are recorded on the phone as not held), and goes live within seconds. The rest flows over the live session in both directions.
- Phones crashed in the background on 0.1.32 ("Must have USE_BIOMETRIC permission") when the app asked whether a fingerprint is set up. The app now declares that permission, and if asking still fails it counts as "no fingerprint", so protected actions use the screen lock or the in-app confirmation.
- Installing an update on a computer said "Monitor App restarts" but the open window never did: the background service moved to the new build, the window kept running the old one, and the version shown changed under it. Install now follows the service through its restart, then restarts the window on the new build (or says why it could not). A window left on an older build says so and offers **Restart Monitor App**; "Downloaded, not installed yet" and "Installed" are worded apart.
- Remove could sit at "Removing … from all your devices…" forever: any failure other than a node error was not caught, and nothing limited the wait. Remove now answers within 45 seconds with the result or the reason, removing a device that is already removed counts as done, and the list reloads either way.
- A phone that scanned a computer's code gave up after about three minutes, so a request accepted later (the computer took up to two minutes to show it) was never finished and the phone stayed removed. The phone now waits the full five minutes the request is open and says "Tap Accept on the computer", a timeout says so in plain words, and the computer's Pairing screen picks up new requests every 3 seconds.
- Phones and tablets with no fingerprint and no screen lock can add devices again: the code check and the Add tap in the app are the confirmation, instead of refusing for a lock that does not exist.

### Added (diagnostics)
- Each computer and phone keeps a diagnostic log of pairing requests and results, connections (each change with the address and the reason it failed), refused devices (for example "removed at …; pair again"), removals, mesh changes and update checks. Computers write one file per day to `logs` in their data folder (kept a week) and show the newest lines at `http://127.0.0.1:55432/api/v1/diagnostics/log`. Keys and other long secrets are cut to their first eight characters.

- Updates got stuck on an older build: once a build was downloaded and waiting for approval, neither the hourly check nor the Check button looked for anything newer, so a device could sit on 0.1.27 while 0.1.30 was out. A newer release now replaces the waiting one (the old download is removed), the same one is kept without downloading again, and Check for updates always says what it found or why it did not check yet.
- A device added on a phone (scan its code) could stay unknown to the computers. A computer that had not yet sent anything about its own pairings ignored everything its directly paired phone told it, and nothing retried. It now vouches for its own pairings before checking what arrives, and a phone renews the session when it has news of its own to pass on.
- Phone and computers stopped exchanging readings on 0.1.18 (T080 hotfix): a computer's advertised address (its Tailscale address) was stored as the newest and always dialed first, so a phone that could not reach it never connected. Devices now dial the address that last worked first, rank advertised addresses below paired ones, and move to the next address when one keeps failing.
- A newly paired phone or tablet starts syncing at once instead of at the next pass (up to 15 seconds, more after a failed try), and a mesh trust change wakes sessions too.
- The phone shows a crash instead of closing, and keeps the details for the next launch (from the shared model thread).

- Saving readings from a paired device wrote one database transaction per reading, so a busy computer took seconds per collection; they are now saved in one batch.
- Menus were laggy again. On the phone every read of its own database (readings, peers, alerts, Things) ran on the screen thread because SQLite's async calls complete synchronously; they now run in the background. The shared model is no longer rebuilt from its whole change log on every read (it is cached until the next change), and Things no longer rebuilds every card, or re-opens the exploded view, when a refresh changed nothing.

- Open it up on Windows printed "Links up:" followed by every driver filter and hidden adapter Windows lists (QoS Packet Scheduler, WFP filters, "Local Area Connection*", 6to4, Teredo) as one line across the screen. Windows no longer collects that plumbing at all, a device with many links names three and counts the rest, the part labels stop at a fixed width with "…", and readings that are unavailable are no longer listed under a part.
- Pairing a phone by QR failed at random: the reader missed about 1 in 20 pairing codes even when they were perfectly sharp. A frame it cannot read now gets a second pass as an upright code, and a test reads 200 random codes.

- Plain readings everywhere (T062): every signal shows a plain label and unit from an extensible `SignalCatalog` (exact ids and `{placeholder}` patterns registered as data, e.g. `lan.link.{interface}.up` reads "Wi-Fi link: Up", `collector.health.battery` reads "Battery readings: Working"); unknown ids read as words, never raw. Readings show "Updated 18:14:22" instead of quality, freshness and source codes. A reading a device does not provide reads "Unavailable" once (not "Unavailable (Unavailable)") and no longer marks a working device Degraded; permission and read errors still do. Network link readings moved from "Other signals" to Connectivity; collector status has its own "Monitoring status" layer. Part pages no longer repeat their summary three times and fold unavailable readings behind "Show N readings this device doesn't provide". History pickers and observation cards use the same labels; alert pages no longer print rule ids.
- A full battery no longer shows as an error (T062): battery gauges are critical at 10% or less and low at 20% or less, and read "96% charged" instead of the storage-style "96% full".
- Menus open without the pause (T062): the shell keeps each mode's page alive and switches visibility instead of rebuilding it on every tap, and Android release builds are fully compiled ahead of time instead of compiling each screen on first use.

### Security

- Alert delivery requires an authenticated, actively trusted PeerSession; a recipient receives only outbox rows addressed to its own peer id, a revoked or unpaired peer receives nothing and cannot acknowledge, acknowledgements cannot exceed an issued revision, and a phone rejects alerts whose origin is not the authenticated desktop. Alert text is bounded and carries rule and subject identifiers only, never credentials.
- Established initial trust boundaries and enrollment requirements before implementation.
- Replaced reusable raw agent credentials in enrollment responses with device public-key proof, SHA-256 fingerprints, and short-lived signed authentication challenges.
- Added structural rejection of malformed or wrong-curve public keys, incorrect secret/challenge/signature/fingerprint sizes, invalid challenge windows, and safe-error secret echo.
- Enrollment secrets are persisted only as per-record salted HMAC-SHA-256 verifiers with a required controller pepper; completion and device challenges are atomically single-use.
- Owner enrollment operations require an administrator token, and controller startup fails closed when bootstrap secrets are missing or weak.
- Update eligibility now fails closed for invalid signatures, untrusted keys, downgrade/reinstall, incompatible versions, wrong targets, and size/hash mismatch; signing and Android-keystore files are excluded from source control.
- Android enrollment retains the private P-256 key inside Android Keystore and never persists the one-time enrollment secret or proof challenge.
- Short pairing codes are one-time, 10-minute, never transmitted (PBKDF2-derived lookup id; the desktop reply carries an HMAC proof keyed by the code), globally rate-limited, and refuse unknown/expired/used/limited attempts identically; see `docs/SECURITY.md` Phone pairing.
